## What actually happened

Record the sequence, written-test questions, interview questions and follow-ups. Preserve the user's original wording when useful. Clearly distinguish exact recollection from approximate memory.

## Evidence performance

For each important exchange, record:

- question and assessment intent;
- evidence used;
- what the user answered;
- interviewer follow-up or reaction;
- what created trust;
- what became vague, unsupported or inconsistent;
- a stronger truthful strategy for next time.

## Candidate experience

- What helped the user perform well?
- Where did anxiety, pace, language or environment affect delivery?
- What did the interview reveal about the team's expectations and working style?

## Two-way fit update

- Did the interview strengthen or weaken the user's interest?
- What new value or risk did the role reveal?
- Which questions remain unanswered?

## Updates

### Application record

Facts specific to this company, role and process.

### Personal evidence bank

### Skill iteration candidate

Only method-level lessons that may improve future applications. Mark whether the lesson has been observed once or repeatedly; do not turn one anecdote into a universal rule.
