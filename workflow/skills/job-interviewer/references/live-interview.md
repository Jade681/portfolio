# Live Interview Protocol

## Opening

Briefly state the role, expected duration, structure and whether notes or recording are used. In a simulation, say feedback will be held until the end. Do not reveal the private scorecard or ideal evidence.

## Run one turn at a time

1. Ask one Level 1 question and let the candidate establish one story, artifact or scenario.
2. Wait for the complete answer.
3. Update the private evidence ledger: claim, concrete evidence, uncertainty, contradiction and next probe.
4. Ask the most informative Level 2 follow-up while explicitly staying in that same story. Only after the same-story evidence is insufficient, ask a Level 3 question using a different independent story or equivalent scenario. Move on when the stop condition is met.
5. Use neutral transitions. Do not coach or score aloud.

Favor follow-ups that distinguish between competing interpretations. If “we” obscures ownership, ask what the candidate personally decided or delivered. If an answer has only conclusions, request a specific situation. If results are claimed, ask how they were measured. If an answer changes, ask the candidate to reconcile versions without accusing them of lying.

## Evidence signals

Possible positive signals include concrete scale/timeline/result, clear personal contribution, a coherent situation–reasoning–action–result chain, honest uncertainty or failure, reflection on alternatives, and role-relevant motivation.

Possible risk signals requiring a probe include contradiction, repeated abstraction, evasion of ownership/result/data/failure, absolute claims without lived detail, unexplained attribution of team or AI output, and inability to explain a central claim at role-appropriate depth.

These are prompts for verification, not automatic personality judgments. Nervousness, brevity, eye contact, speaking style or accent alone are not competence evidence. Fluent delivery is also not proof: continue until the candidate's actual actions and decisions are visible.

If the candidate has little formal experience, accept small but real examples such as a class project, sustained interest, club task or self-initiated action. Judge what the candidate did, not the prestige or size of the event.

## Candidate-question segment

Before the final close, reserve time for the candidate to ask questions whenever this matches the real process. State the transition clearly: the assessment questions are complete; candidate questions no longer change the evidence ledger unless the process explicitly treats them as assessed.

In a real employer interview, answer within the authority and confidentiality boundary. If an answer is unknown, say so rather than inventing policy or commitments.

In a candidate-practice simulation, role-play the interviewer and let the user ask one question at a time. After the candidate signals they are finished, briefly assess the questions separately from the interview performance:

- whether each question is specific enough to produce useful information;
- whether it helps the candidate judge the role, manager, priorities, success criteria, team workflow or next steps;
- whether it follows naturally from information already shared, instead of asking for facts available in the job description;
- whether it avoids premature salary, benefits or confidential internal-comparison questions when those would be poorly timed for that stage.

Offer one or two stronger alternatives only after the simulation has ended. Do not require a fixed number of questions: one well-chosen question is preferable to a long list.

## Time control and close

Spend time in proportion to scorecard weight and uncertainty. A 20–30 minute interview can use a brief opening, 3–4 high-priority dimensions, candidate questions and close. Do not exhaust every follow-up on the first story while missing a mandatory dimension.

If time is nearly over, ask the highest-information remaining question and record unassessed criteria. Ask whether the candidate wants to add relevant evidence, run the candidate-question segment when appropriate, explain next steps if known, then end. Only after an explicit end marker switch to evaluation mode.

If the user says “结束面试”, “停止模拟” or equivalent, stop asking questions and evaluate collected evidence. Mark uncovered dimensions unassessed. If candidate feedback is part of the process, end the assessment explicitly, synthesize evidence, then switch to the feedback protocol; do not let feedback change the already-recorded interview evidence.
