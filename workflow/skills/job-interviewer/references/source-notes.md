# Source Notes and Iteration Boundaries

## Sources used for the initial version

- Local `job-application-copilot` Skill and interview-preparation contract.
- Local AI product internship test report, iteration log, evidence inventory, targeted resume draft and master resume information bank.
- Four Polaris Youth sources supplied by the user and read on 2026-08-28: the interview collaboration document, interviewer guide, candidate-information/execution page and interview-record spreadsheet. Together they supplied observed practices for defining a candidate profile, preparing from candidate materials, assigning panel roles, using Level 1/2/3 questions, protecting candidate information and recording links, recording positive/risk evidence, checking missing dimensions, applying anchored scores and completing a traceable post-interview record.

## Generalized versus case-specific

Generalized: begin with role and observable traits; establish the claim, deepen the same experience, then independently re-verify in another context when still uncertain; give inexperienced candidates fair opportunities to show small but real actions; separate signals from raw evidence; compare interviewer impressions against evidence; assign panel roles; protect privacy and interview flow; check omissions before closing; evaluate afterward.

Case-specific: Polaris Youth's four traits (self-drive, leadership, consistency and thinking), panel size, speaking limits, question count, duration and observed candidate answers. Reuse only when supported by the new role.

## Future iteration rule

After a real interview or simulation, separate case notes, candidate evidence updates and reusable method changes. Change this Skill only when a failure reveals a general decision problem or repeats across cases. Record the observation, prior behavior, narrow change, expected effect and remaining validation.

## External calibration added 2026-08-29

Current skills-first and structured-assessment guidance reinforced these design choices:

- U.S. OPM structured-interview guidance: job analysis, shared job-related competencies, standardized core questions and anchored scoring improve validity and rater agreement.
- OPM assessment-strategy guidance: work samples and structured interviews are strong options when they represent actual work.
- OECD and World Economic Forum 2025–2026 reporting: employers increasingly emphasize demonstrated skills and direct assessment rather than relying only on degrees or resume claims.
- Current university event-coordinator JDs: recurring work includes stakeholder coordination, venue/accessibility/AV/logistics, budgets and invoices, timelines, risk and emergency planning, day-of troubleshooting, metrics and post-event evaluation.

Use these as method and task-pattern evidence, not universal scorecards. Refresh external research when a role, market or tool environment may have changed.
